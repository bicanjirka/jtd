## 1. The game

jTD is a single-player desktop tower-defense game in Java and Swing. Enemies walk one or more
fixed paths across a grid board. The player spends credits on towers placed on the cells beside
the paths and tries to kill every enemy before it reaches a path's end, where it costs a life.

- Three built-in levels, eight towers, seven built-in enemy types plus a boss chain and one
  level-specific enemy, five enemy ranks, eleven status effects, two damage types.
- Every tower has a three-slot upgrade tree whose nodes are unlocked by price and by the
  tower's own record (kills, damage dealt, nearby towers).
- All artwork is vector, drawn in code. There are no image assets.
- The simulation is deterministic given a seed, and fully headless: tests, the balance harness
  and the performance harness drive the same engine the window does.

## 2. Game flow

1. **Level select.** The game opens on a menu with one card per level showing a path preview,
   the wave count, starting credits and starting lives. Clicking a card starts that level.
2. **Build phase.** Nothing moves until the player starts the first round. The player places
   towers and buys upgrades at any time, between rounds or during one.
3. **Rounds.** `s` or the ▶ button starts the next round. A round spawns every path's wave for
   that index together. Rounds never auto-advance: the next can start only after every enemy of
   the current round is dead or has leaked.
4. **Outcome.** The level is **lost** the moment lives reach zero, checked before the win, so a
   last enemy leaking the last life loses. It is **won** when the final round is cleared. A
   small banner with a "Back to menu" button appears over the board, which stays inspectable
   but can no longer change.
5. **Back to menu.** `m` returns to the menu at any time (with a confirmation mid-level). Any
   level can then be picked again with no state carried over.

Game speed: pause, normal, fast (×3.3) and super fast (×16.7), from the ▶ / ❚❚ / ▶▶ / ▶▶▶
buttons or `p` (pause) and `f` (cycle). ▶▶ while paused advances a single tick. The board keeps
redrawing while paused.

## 3. Economy

The ledger holds **credits**, **score** and **lives**.

| Event          | Credits                                   | Score                         | Lives |
|----------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-------|
| Level start    | the level's starting credits              | 0                             | the level's starting lives |
| Buy a tower    | − tower price                             | —                             | —     |
| Buy an upgrade | − node price                              | —                             | —     |
| Sell a tower   | + 75% of (tower price + upgrades bought)  | —                             | —     |
| Kill an enemy  | + its bounty                              | + bounty × rank multiplier    | —     |
| Enemy leaks    | —                                         | − its bounty                  | −1    |

- Rank score multipliers: Grunt ×1.0, Soldier ×1.3, Veteran ×1.7, Elite ×2.2, Boss ×3.0.
- A buy is an atomic check-and-charge: it either charges and succeeds or does nothing.
- An egg hatching into the next boss stage is a replacement, not a kill: it pays no bounty.
- Swarm members split their slot's bounty exactly, fixed at spawn.

## 4. Towers

Range is in cells. Damage is in points. The game ticks 20 times a second, and a tower with
cooldown *c* attacks every *c* + 1 ticks.

| Tower  | Key | Price | Damage            | Type     | Range | Attack rate        | Aims at                                    |
|--------|-----|-------|-------------------|----------|-------|--------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Sniper | `q` | 10    | 30                | physical | 3.8   | 0.5 / s            | visible enemy furthest along its path      |
| Splash | `w` | 15    | 16, with falloff  | physical | 3.2   | 1.0 / s            | random visible enemy                       |
| Sonar  | `e` | 20    | 16 per beam pass  | physical | 5.2   | one sweep per 2 s  | every visible enemy the beam passes        |
| Pulse  | `r` | 25    | 2 per tick        | physical | 1.5   | every tick         | everything in range                        |
| Aura   | `t` | 20    | —                 | —        | 1.5   | passive            | other towers in range                      |
| Mortar | `y` | 30    | 20, with falloff  | physical | 4.0   | 0.39 / s           | visible enemy furthest along its path      |
| Seeker | `u` | 35    | 26                | magic    | 4.5   | 0.43 / s           | visible enemy furthest along its path      |
| Cinder | `i` | 28    | 1.5 burn per tick | magic    | 2.2   | 0.95 / s           | nearest visible enemy                      |

- **Sniper.** Single target; 15% innate crit chance. Its turret turns at a capped rate and
  holds its heading when idle. Any special upgrade switches it to the enemy with the most
  health left.
- **Splash.** Damages everything within a 1.75-cell blast of its target, falling off as
  `1 − (d / radius)²`. The blast also hits invisible enemies.
- **Sonar.** No cooldown: a beam sweeps the full circle every 2 s and hits every visible enemy
  in the arc swept since the last tick, so enemies on one bearing are all hit together.
- **Pulse.** Damages everything in range every tick, invisible enemies included, but only while
  a visible enemy is in range, so it never gives away a hidden one on its own.
- **Aura.** Never attacks. Each tower whose centre is in its range gets +20% damage and +20%
  range; several auras stack additively. It does not buff other auras unless upgraded, and the
  board draws a faint link to every tower it buffs.
- **Mortar.** Lobs an unguided shell to where the target was when fired, so fast enemies can
  dodge it. The 2-cell blast falls off with distance and chills everything it reaches (50%
  for 2 s).
- **Seeker.** Fires a homing missile that re-aims every tick and retargets the nearest enemy if
  its target dies. On impact it deals magic damage and freezes whatever it actually hit for
  1.5 s.
- **Cinder.** Turns slowly toward the nearest visible enemy and fires once an enemy is inside
  its cone (about 40° wide). Each shot is a wave travelling outward over 10 ticks that sets
  every enemy it reaches burning for 3 s, invisible ones included. It deals no direct damage;
  its burns credit it.

A tower's record (kills, damage that actually landed) is tracked per tower and drives its
upgrade gates. Damage from a burn or poison keeps crediting the tower that applied it, until
that tower is sold.

## 5. Tower upgrades

Every tower has its own **upgrade tree** of three slots. A placed tower's tree is bought node by
node and is permanent for that tower.

| Slot      | Holds                                                    | Rule                                                        |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| `BASE`    | **Range** (+15% range) and **Awaken**                    | both bought independently, gated on price only              |
| `HEAD`    | two exclusive chains of 2-3 levels                       | needs Awaken; buying one chain's root closes the other      |
| `SPECIAL` | one to three exclusive specialisations                   | needs Awaken; buying one closes the rest                    |

A node is **offered** once its prerequisites are owned, and **buyable** once its **gate** is met
and it is affordable. Gates are per tower:

- price only;
- *N* kills by this tower;
- *N* points of damage landed by this tower;
- cluster: at least *N* other towers in the 8 cells around it.

The upgrade panel shows each offered node with its price or its gate progress (`7/10 kills`).
On the board each tower carries one pip mark per slot (owned levels, and a highlight when a node
is ready to buy), and a tower with a special glows with a pulsing halo.

Base-slot prices (Range / Awaken): Sniper 6 / 10, Splash 9 / 15, Sonar 12 / 20, Pulse 15 / 25,
Aura 12 / 20, Mortar 18 / 30, Seeker 21 / 35, Cinder 17 / 28.

Percentages below are bonuses to the tower's own stats. Fire-rate bonuses cut the remaining
cooldown, so they multiply rather than add.

### Buffs and disruption

A tower's current stats are its base stats folded with every buff it receives: nearby auras,
its own upgrade nodes, a timed self-buff (Momentum) and enemy disruption. Buff axes are damage,
range, fire rate, bounty, crit chance, armor penetration and magic penetration. Bonuses add up,
except fire rate, where each bonus cuts the cooldown that remains. Combined fire-rate and range
bonuses never fall below −75%, so no stack of penalties stalls a tower.

## 6. Combat model

**Units.** Health, damage, plating and healing are authored and displayed in points and stored
internally in hundredths of a point, so a percentage or a damage-over-time pulse can remove part
of a point without rounding it away.

**Damage types.** Every hit is **physical** or **magic**. Each type has its own armor (armor /
magic resist), its own plating and its own damage-taken multiplier on the enemy.

**How a hit lands**, in this order, computed once and rounded once:

1. **Crit roll.** Chance = attacker's crit chance × the enemy's crit-chance-taken × (1 −
   resilience / 100). A crit multiplies damage by 1 + (multiplier − 1) × (1 − resilience / 100).
   The default multiplier is 1.5×. Resilience 100 is crit-immune; negative resilience makes crits
   hit harder but never likelier.
2. **Mitigation.** The attacker's penetration first lowers positive armor (percentage, then
   flat). Damage is then multiplied by `100 / (100 + armor)`; negative armor mirrors it, up to
   double damage.
3. **Plating.** A flat number of points off the hit (reduced by plating penetration), never
   below zero.
4. **Damage taken.** Multiplied by the enemy's damage-taken for that type (Vulnerable and
   shields act here; floored at 10%).
5. **Cap** at the enemy's remaining health. The tower is credited with what landed.

**Adaptive armor.** The level tallies every point of damage landed, by type. Some enemies pick
their armor once, at spawn, from that mix: none at an even split, rising linearly to full
strength when every point so far was one type. Elite and Boss Simple and Elite+ Mender enemies
resist whichever type dominates (up to 40% resisted). The `armored` spawn shape adds
physical-only armor from 20% at an even split up to 80% under an all-physical defence.

**Crit immunity** is resilience 100: Armored enemies, the Boss Frenzied and the first Warden egg
have it.

## 7. Status effects

Towers and enemy abilities put timed effects on enemies. An enemy shows up to three as markers on
the board and the rest as "+N"; selecting it lists every effect, its kind and the time left.

| Effect     | Kind             | What it does                                                                                       |
|------------|------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Chilled    | soft CC          | Slows by its level, which adds up across applications (never past 80%) and fades linearly. Cuts burn damage taken, by up to half at the cap |
| Freeze     | hard CC          | Stops the enemy; it cannot cast abilities while frozen. Diminishes: within 10 s of the last freeze, each fresh one lasts half as long, and a fourth doesn't take hold. Freezing a chilled enemy consumes the chill and lasts longer by its level |
| Burning    | damage over time | A decaying pool of magic damage; several towers add to one pool and each is credited its share. Earns a Scorched stack when it starts and every half second while it lasts |
| Poisoned   | damage over time | Its own decaying pool, so it stacks with burning. Slows in proportion to the pool (at most 30%, on top of chill). Earns Sickened stacks the same way |
| Scorched   | debuff           | Each stack lowers resilience by 1 (down to −100). Outlasts the fire; loses a stack per second       |
| Sickened   | debuff           | Each stack lowers spirit by 1 (down to −100), shrinking heals and shields to nothing at the floor  |
| Vulnerable | debuff           | Up to 3 stacks, +15% damage taken each, from any tower, on one shared 4 s clock any application refreshes |
| Shield     | restorative      | Absorbs a share of every hit (can be restricted to one damage type)                                |
| Heal       | restorative      | Restores health every tick, capped at max health                                                   |
| Invisible  | stealth          | Towers cannot aim at the enemy; area damage still reaches it                                       |
| Revealed   | stealth          | Towers can aim at the enemy again, even through invisibility                                       |

- Burn and poison damage lands in pulses four times a second; the pool decays every tick, and a
  top-up adds less the fuller the pool already is.
- Debuff stacks wear off at a pace set by spirit: neutral spirit loses one per second, more spirit
  is faster, and at −100 they never wear off.
- Interactions: a frozen enemy cannot burn; freezing puts out a burn but keeps its Scorched
  stacks. Nothing else cancels anything.
- Shields and heals are scaled by spirit (`max(0, 1 + spirit / 100)`).
- Resistances (chill, burn, freeze) only shorten an effect's duration; an effect shortened below
  one tick is blocked.

**Spawned-only enemies.**

- **Frenzy Spawnling** (`tSpawn`): 140 health, bounty 2, speeds up when hurt (×1.4), and heals
  allies within 150 px as it dies.

**The Warden** (`warden1`), a three-stage boss chain, the final round of Curly Path:

| Stage                | Health | Bounty | Speed | Physical plating |
|----------------------|--------|--------|-------|------------------|
| The Warden           | 8000   | 100    | 0.8   | 10               |
| The Weakened Warden  | 5000   | 100    | 0.8   | 5                |
| The Exhausted Warden | 3000   | 100    | 0.8   | 2.5              |

Every stage summons a Simple every 15 s, shields itself every 20 s (50% for 5 s), shields
every ally within 150 px once below half health (30% for 7.5 s), heals itself when unhit for
10 s, and shields itself (30% for 1.5 s) whenever it survives a critical hit. On death it lays a
stationary egg (1500 health, bounty 20) that hatches into the next stage after 8 s unless
destroyed. The first egg is crit-immune with 40% armor; the second is immune to burn and freeze
with 1 point of plating; the final egg never hatches and must be destroyed to end the fight.

**Reaver** (`reaver`), registered only for Twisted Hourglass: 625 / 1250 / 2500 / 5000 / 10000
health, bounty 12 / 20 / 32 / 50 / 80, speed 1.28, faces along the path. Speeds up when hurt
(×1.3), shrugs off 0.1 points of every hit, shields itself (25% for 5 s) once below half health,
and splits into two Simple enemies when it dies.

**Ranks.** Grunt, Soldier, Veteran, Elite, Boss. Health and bounty rise with rank, and the score
of a kill is weighted by it. A rank is drawn as a badge on the body, not as a number. Asking for a
rank an enemy does not define uses its highest defined rank.

**Movement.** Enemies follow the path by arc length, so they move at a steady real pace on
curves and straights alike. Formation members keep their offset from the path centre, and
enemies walk in from and out to off-board.

## 9. Waves and levels

### Wave script

A wave is a line of space-separated tokens:

- an enemy token (`c s t g m j e warden1`, or a level's own ids such as `reaver`);
- a **count** before a token repeats it: `3 s e 4 c`; before a rank or shape it repeats the
  whole slot: `3 elite swarm 4 c`;
- a **rank** keyword (`grunt soldier veteran elite boss`) overrides the wave's default rank for
  one slot;
- a **shape** keyword before the enemy changes how the slot spawns:

| Shape   | Keyword   | One slot produces                                                                 |
|---------|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Normal  | (none)    | one enemy on the path centre                                                       |
| Armored | `armored` | one enemy with extra physical-only adaptive armor (20% → 80%)                      |
| Swarm   | `swarm n` | *n* enemies at half size, scattered off-path, sharing one enemy's health and bounty |
| Line    | `line n`  | *n* enemies spread evenly across the path, abreast                                 |
| Flank   | `flank`   | two enemies hugging opposite edges of the path                                     |
| Column  | `column n`| *n* enemies in tight single file                                                   |
| Drip    | `drip n`  | *n* enemies stretched over more time than *n* separate slots                       |

A shape takes 1-12 members. A malformed script (unknown token, misplaced rank or count) stops the
game at startup with a clear error. Formation scatter is seeded from the level and wave, so it
reproduces.

### Levels

A level is a board size, one or more coloured paths each with its own waves, starting credits
and lives, a corner-smoothing style, and optionally its own enemies. Every path in a level has
the same number of waves; round *N* spawns wave *N* of every path. Cells the final (curved) path
geometry covers are unbuildable.

| Level             | Board  | Paths                         | Rounds | Credits | Lives | Smoothing        | Notes |
|-------------------|--------|-------------------------------|--------|---------|-------|------------------|-------|
| Curly Path        | 20×13  | 1                             | 18     | 50      | 5     | circular arcs    | One lane through two tight loops into a zigzag; ends with the Warden |
| Zigzag Path       | 16×11  | 2 (fuchsia, lime)             | 8      | 75      | 3     | circular arcs    | Fuchsia hooks back on itself; lime cuts across it twice |
| Twisted Hourglass | 9×14   | 3 (crimson, teal, amber)      | 10     | 100     | 4     | quadratic Bézier | Three lanes pinched through a narrow waist; introduces the Reaver and every spawn shape |

Levels are Java constants in `td.level`; there is no level file format yet.

## 10. Interface and controls

**Screens.** A level-select menu and the game screen, switched in one window. The game screen
holds the board, a tower toolbar, a console (version, round `n/total`, lives, score, cash and the
speed buttons), an info pane, and a pane that shows the wave preview, or the upgrade tree while a
tower is selected.

**Board.** Path trails with moving chevrons in each path's colour; tower bodies (one flat symbol
per tower) with animated turret heads; beams, blasts, pulses, cones, shells and missiles; enemy
bodies with rank badges, up to three status markers, trait markers, shield bubbles, support-aura
rings, ability-cast and spawn pulses, crit sparks, an ice crystal on frozen enemies, a cloak fade
for invisibility and a death fade. Placement shows a highlight on the hovered cell.

**Inspection.**

- Hovering a toolbar tower shows its shop sheet: price, stats and what it does.
- Clicking a placed tower selects it: its stats (base → current), behaviours, kills, damage
  dealt and a **Sell** button with the refund; the upgrade tree replaces the wave preview.
  Hovering an upgrade node describes it.
- Clicking an enemy shows its live health, stats, effects and traits, updating even while
  paused, and keeps a "Killed" or "Leaked" line after it is gone until the next click.
- The wave preview shows the current and next round, one row per path, drawn as real enemies;
  hovering one shows that enemy type's stat block.

**Controls.**

|---------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `q` `w` `e` `r` `t` `y` `u` `i` | Pick tower 1-8 for placement                                            |

## 11. Playtesting and developer tools

- **Debug keys** `n`, `x`, `c` are always on, to make manual playtesting cheap.
- **`td.BalanceHarness`** plays a level headless with a fixed tower loadout and no input, and
  reports lives lost, ticks to clear each round, and each tower's kills and damage.
- **`td.PerformanceHarness`** fills every buildable cell of the last built-in level with towers,
  plays it once to warm up and five more times measured, and reports p50/p99/max tick time,
  frame-build time and bytes allocated per frame build. It exits non-zero over budget.
- **`PreviewBoard` / `PreviewEnemy`** (in the `run-jtd` Claude Code skill) render a board or a
  single enemy scene straight to PNG without a window, for checking visual changes.
- **Logging.** SLF4J + Logback. Each run writes `logs/jTD-<timestamp>.log` plus console output at
  `INFO`; per-tick detail is at `DEBUG`, where an ASCII renderer also dumps the board each tick.
  Old run logs are pruned at startup.

## 12. Technical architecture

**Stack.** Java 25, Swing/Java2D, Maven. Runtime dependencies: SLF4J and Logback only. Tests:
JUnit 5 and AssertJ. 277 main source files (about 18,300 lines) and 118 test files (about 13,000
lines, 819 tests).

**Packages** (`td.*`): `board` (scale, cell↔pixel math) · `cell` (squares, buildability) ·
`damage` · `economy` · `effect` (timed status effects) · `enemy` · `level` · `projectile` ·
`stat` (enemy stat sheet) · `tower` (+ `targeting`, `buff`, `upgrade`) · `ui` (+ `render`) ·
`util` (`GameWorld`, `GameHost`, threading helpers) · `wave` (path geometry, smoothing, wave
scripts). `GameEngine`, `GameLoop`, `TowerDefense` and `Main` sit in `td`.

**Headless engine.** `GameEngine` and the domain packages own all game state and input
semantics and never touch Swing/AWT. `TowerDefense` (the `JFrame`) and `td.ui` own presentation.
`GameHost` is the engine's only channel back to the UI. Every rule is therefore testable without
a display, and `GameEngineTest` is the integration surface.

**Composition.** `GameWorld` hands out focused collaborators (`economy()`, `enemies()`,
`towers()`, `projectiles()`, `waves()`, `level()`), and a consumer depends on the narrowest one
it needs. A level load publishes one immutable `LoadedLevel`; `loadLevel` is idempotent, so the
round trip to the menu needs no separate cleanup.

**Simulation loop.** A dedicated `game-loop` thread runs a fixed 20 ticks/s timestep scaled by
the speed multiplier, plus a ~60 fps frame build that keeps running while paused. Each tick runs
enemies, then projectiles, then towers, then settles deaths and the round state. Falling behind
runs one tick and resyncs rather than bursting. A per-tick failure is logged and skipped; ten in
a row stop the loop.

**Render pipeline.** `BoardRenderer.buildFrame` runs on the loop thread and describes the board
as an immutable, AWT-free `RenderFrame` of draw records, one frame builder per domain type
dispatching through visitors. `Java2DFrameRenderer` alone turns it into pixels on the EDT;
`AsciiBoardRenderer` is a second, text backend. Two clocks drive animation: tick interpolation
for anything backed by simulation state, and wall-clock seconds for pure cosmetics.

**Threading.** The thread that owns a piece of state publishes it; the other reads only what was
published. Correlated fields cross as one immutable snapshot behind one `volatile`
(`RenderFrame`, `LoadedLevel`, `TowerStats`, enemy inspections). The economy computes under a
lock and notifies outside it. Live-entity and listener lists are copy-on-write.

**Data-driven content.** Enemies are `EnemyDefinition`s composed of traits and abilities, with
rank ladders and per-level registration; a new enemy is a definition, not a class. Towers
compose targeting filters and selectors; upgrade trees are data built from nodes, conditions and
buffs. Stats resolve through one stat-sheet formula; hits through one hit-resolution formula.

**Value algebras.** Values that combine have an operation, an identity and, where useful, an
absorber: `Damage`, `EconomyDelta`, `TowerBuff`, `StatModifier`, `TargetQuery`, path smoothing.
Absence is a value (`none()`, `empty()`), never `null`.

**Determinism.** All randomness comes from an injected `RandomSource`, so tests and balance runs
reproduce.

**Path geometry.** Authored as cell corners, converted to pixel points, smoothed by a per-level
strategy (circular arc or quadratic Bézier fillets), and walked by arc length. Buildability is
computed by supersampling the final geometry.

**Performance budget** (p99, last built-in level, every buildable cell a tower): tick ≤ 1 ms,
frame build ≤ 1 ms, ≤ 512 KB allocated per frame build. Tests: 5 s each, 60 s per fork.

## 13. Build, quality gates and release

- `mvn verify` runs Spotless (formatting), Checkstyle (naming and code-style rules, plus import
  control: no Swing/AWT in the engine packages or in `td.ui.render`) and the test suite.
- `mvn package` builds a shaded `target/jTD.jar` that runs with `java -jar` and needs no
  classpath.
- `pom.xml`'s `<version>` is the single source of the version; it is filtered into the jar and
  shown in the window title.
- Release 1.4 is the first tagged release. Each release is tagged `v<version>` on the commit that
  documents it in `docs/releases/`, and the next commit raises the pom version.

## 14. Known gaps at 1.4

`TODO.md` is the full list. The headline items at this version:

- Tower, upgrade, effect, enemy and crit numbers are unbalanced placeholders.
- Levels exist only as Java code; a file-based level catalog is planned.
- The Jammer exists but appears in no wave; no built-in ability uses a type-restricted shield.
- No global (per tower type) upgrades, and no shared bounty for kills inside an aura.
- Acid, a second damage-over-time effect, is designed but not built.
- The simulation is not yet a strict single writer: buying and selling run on the UI thread, so
  there is no deterministic replay.
- The Warden's in-game description promises an extra reinforcement when left unattacked; the
  ability actually heals it.
- A hatched Warden egg leaves its stale stats in the enemy inspector.
- The second iteration of tower upgrades (Transcendent awaken, a fourth head level, more
  specials) is a feature request, not yet built.
